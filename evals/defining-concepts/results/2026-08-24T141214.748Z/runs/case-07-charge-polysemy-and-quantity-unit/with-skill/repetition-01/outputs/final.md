## 1. Semantic Analysis

- **Designation Evaluated:** Charge
- **Concept Boundary:** The intended concept is electric charge as a measurable physical quantity associated with elementary particles and macroscopic matter. It is distinct from electric current, the coulomb as its measurement unit, charge density, elementary charge, a quantity value, and non-physical senses such as a price, accusation, assignment, or battery state.
- **Superordinate Concept (Genus):** Additive scalar quantity.
- **Delimiting Characteristics (Differentia):** It is associated with elementary particles and macroscopic matter and characterizes their electromagnetic interactions.
- **Designation Precision:** “Charge” is established as a shortened synonym in physics, but it is highly polysemous outside a tightly controlled physics context. For a registry-level designation intended to remain intelligible when displayed independently, **Electric charge** is linguistically and terminologically preferable. Retain “Charge” only as an admitted synonym.

## 2. Standardization & Reuse Check

- **Repositories Queried:** IEC International Electrotechnical Vocabulary/Electropedia; IUPAC Gold Book; BIPM/JCGM International Vocabulary of Metrology; BIPM International System of Units resources.
- **Candidate Evidence:**
  - **Exact definition:** IEC 60050-121:1998, entry 121-11-01, defines *electric charge* using the same genus and intended electrostatic/electromagnetic concept.
  - **Constituent semantics:** The JCGM vocabulary identifies electric charge as a quantity, describes quantities as scalar in its conceptual model, and distinguishes a quantity from its value and unit.
  - **Constituent semantics:** BIPM identifies the coulomb as related to the ampere and second by \(C=A\,s\) and assigns an exact SI value to the elementary charge. These statements support representation and unit boundaries but do not constitute the concept definition.
  - **Adapted-definition candidate:** The IUPAC Gold Book search exposed an *electric charge* entry formulated as an integral of electric current over time, but direct retrieval of the destination failed. It therefore did not pass the evidence gate.
- **Action Taken:** **Path A — Reuse.** The IEC definition has the same extension and intension as the requested concept and already satisfies the mandatory ISO/IEC 11179-4 requirements. It is reused without semantic or wording changes.
- **Source Provenance:** The exact definition is from IEC 60050-121:1998, entry 121-11-01, as reproduced in a directly retrieved preview of the IEC publication. The BIPM/JCGM sources provide supporting metrological and unit semantics only.
- **Verification Limitations:** A current individual Electropedia entry was not retrieved, so this assessment does not establish whether IEC has subsequently revised entry 121-11-01. The IUPAC destination returned an access error and is not treated as verified evidence.

## 3. The Formulated Definition

Additive scalar quantity, associated with elementary particles and with macroscopic matter that characterizes their electromagnetic interactions.

## 4. ISO/IEC 11179-4 Compliance Audit

- **Mandatory Requirements:**
  - **Singularity:** Pass — the definition states one quantity concept in the singular.
  - **Positive declaration:** Pass — it states what the quantity is and what it characterizes.
  - **Descriptive form:** Pass — it is a descriptive noun phrase headed by the genus “additive scalar quantity.”
  - **Abbreviations:** Pass — it contains no abbreviations.
  - **Embedded definitions:** Pass — it does not attempt to define scalar quantity, elementary particle, matter, or electromagnetic interaction internally.
- **Ontological Category Check:** Pass — the definition identifies a quantity, not its value, symbol, measurement unit, permissible-value set, carrier, distribution, measurement procedure, electric current, or battery condition. Elementary particles and matter are bearers of the quantity, not the quantity itself.
- **Refinement Notes:** No starter definition required correction. The substantive registry refinement concerns the designation: replace the preferred designation “Charge” with **“Electric charge”**, while retaining “Charge” as a context-dependent synonym.
