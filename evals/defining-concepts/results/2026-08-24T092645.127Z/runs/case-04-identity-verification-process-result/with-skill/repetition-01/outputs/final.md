### 1. Semantic Analysis

* Designation Evaluated: Identity Verification Outcome
* Superordinate Concept (Genus): Determination
* Delimiting Characteristics (Differentia): Reached by an identity-verification process and concerning the association between an individual and presented identity evidence.
* Etymological Justification: “Identity” derives ultimately from Latin *idem*, meaning “the same”; “verification” denotes establishing authenticity or truth; and “outcome” denotes that which results from something. The designation therefore accurately identifies a determination produced by verification, rather than the verification process itself.

### 2. Standardization & Reuse Check

* Repositories Queried: ISO Online Browsing Platform, NIST Digital Identity Guidelines, W3C standards resources, and the Financial Industry Business Ontology.
* Action Taken: Path C — a new definition was formulated. No exact reusable definition for “Identity Verification Outcome” was located. NIST’s treatment of identity verification supplied the authoritative domain semantics; the generic ISO definition of “outcome” was too specific to artificial-intelligence decision candidates for reuse.
* Source Provenance: The formulation adopts NIST’s conception of identity verification as establishing the association between an individual and presented identity evidence, while modeling the outcome as a determination distinct from both the process and its permissible values.

### 3. The Formulated Definition

A determination reached by an identity-verification process concerning the association between an individual and presented identity evidence.

### 4. ISO/IEC 11179-4 Compliance Audit

* Requirement Check: Pass — singular; positive; descriptive phrase; free of abbreviations; atomic; concise; standalone; non-circular; and expressed through genus and delimiting characteristics.
* Refinement Notes: The starter definition’s process-category error was removed: the concept is a determination produced by a process, not a process that performs checks. Operational behavior and the value labels “Pass,” “Fail,” and “Inconclusive” were excluded because they describe processing logic and a separate value domain.
