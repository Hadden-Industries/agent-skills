### 1. Semantic Analysis

* Designation Evaluated: Charge
* Superordinate Concept (Genus): Scalar physical quantity
* Delimiting Characteristics (Differentia): It belongs to a physical system; its change over a time interval equals the time integral of the net electric current entering that system; its quantity value may be positive, negative, or zero.
* Etymological Justification: *Charge* derives through Anglo-French *charger* from Late Latin *carricāre*, “to load a wagon.” The electrical sense metaphorically construes a body as carrying a load and has been recorded in English since the eighteenth century. This origin appropriately motivates the physical sense but does not uniquely identify it: the same load-or-burden metaphor generated the financial, legal, custodial, and task-related senses. The unqualified designation is therefore insufficiently precise for a domain-independent metadata registry. **Electric charge** is the recommended preferred designation; **charge** may be retained as an admitted synonym or displayed short label within an explicitly scoped physics glossary.

### 2. Standardization & Reuse Check

* Repositories Queried: IUPAC Gold Book, IEC Electropedia, BIPM International System of Units documentation, ISO Online Browsing Platform, BARTOC-indexed vocabulary search, and general English etymological and lexical sources.
* Action Taken: **Path B — Adapt.** IUPAC defines *electric charge* as the integral of electric current over time and identifies *charge* as a synonym. IEC provides the same integral relationship for electric charge in circuit theory. The standardized formulation below adds the immediate genus, identifies the bearer as a physical system, and expresses the integral relationship as a change in the system’s quantity value. These changes make the definition independently intelligible and avoid restricting it to a circuit terminal.
* Source Provenance: The defining differentia was adapted from the IUPAC and IEC formulations. The quantity’s conventional name and synonym relationship come from IUPAC; its metrological treatment and SI unit, the coulomb, are corroborated by BIPM. The designation assessment is supported by the documented polysemy and etymology of *charge*.

### 3. The Formulated Definition

**Electric charge:** Scalar physical quantity of a system whose change over a time interval equals the time integral of the net electric current entering the system.

### 4. ISO/IEC 11179-4 Compliance Audit

* Requirement Check: **Pass.** The definition is singular; states positively what the concept is; forms a complete descriptive phrase; contains no abbreviation; and does not embed a subsidiary definition. It uses the genus–differentia structure required by ISO 704. It is concise, independently interpretable, non-circular, and logically consistent. The bearer is correctly identified as a physical system, while electric charge itself is identified as a quantity; consequently, no material object, stored energy, battery condition, price, accusation, or assigned duty is conflated with the quantity. Recommended registry designation: **Electric charge**. Admitted synonym: **Charge**. Conventional symbols: *Q* or *q*. SI unit: coulomb, symbol C.
* Refinement Notes: The source formulations were converted from a bare mathematical characterization into an intensional definition with an explicit genus. Measurement procedures, electrostatic-force consequences, charge conservation, quantization, elementary-particle examples, and unit-realization details were excluded because they are explanatory or operational rather than essential delimiting characteristics. The phrase “net electric current entering the system” accommodates both inflow and outflow without embedding procedural logic.
